# Sell the referee, not the trade

**Yes, the founder is looking from the wrong angle. Crypto listing-announcement trading is not the best option. Judged as a business rather than as a trade, it is one of the weakest on the table** [Speculative, synthesis]. Three rounds of research, run separately, all reach the same place: every own-capital strategy legally open to a UK-resident solo founder tops out somewhere between **low thousands and low tens of thousands of pounds a year**. That covers listing reactions, Betfair lead-lag, special situations, matched betting and liquidity provision. The reasons are capacity, fees and faster bots, not a lack of effort ([Racing Post](https://racingpost.com/news/britain/betfair-exchange-to-introduce-new-commission-system-for-2025-as-premium-charge-is-dropped-a7wbg0v4GCAJ); [The Tie](https://www.thetie.io/insights/what-a-binance-listing-delivers-for-already-listed-tokens)). The small teams that reached £300k–£10m in this space sold tools and data to people with money at stake. OddsJam was bootstrapped to about **$26M of revenue and $12M of EBITDA** and sold for **$80M plus up to $80M** ([SEC 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839799/000183979925000022/gamb-20241231.htm); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2024/12/12/gamblingcom-buying-oddsjam-parent/)). RebelBetting ran on **four staff at about a 54% EBIT margin** ([FT via euro2day](https://www.euro2day.gr/ftcom_en/article-ft-en/1248302/gambling-software-rebelbetting-enables-punters-to.html)). The founder's real asset is not an edge. It is a **referee**: a data layer that never fabricates, a deterministic rule engine an LLM cannot override, an adversarial critic, and statistics that are gated by sample size. The single recommendation is **Parity**, a working name. It would be a settlement-risk and contract-equivalence data API for prediction-market builders and cross-venue traders outside the UK, answering the question that turns "arbitrage" into one-way losses: *are these two contracts on different venues really the same bet?* It fits the pattern behind every benchmark: a demand wave (Kalshi plus Polymarket volume went roughly tenfold in a year, to about **$50.6B in July 2026**), fragmented venues, buyers who pay $99–$999 a month for data, and consolidators already buying tools ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/web3/2026-08-03-kalshi-polymarket-volume-july-410382); [The Block on Dome](https://theblock.co/post/390546/polymarket-buys-fresh-prediction-market-api-startup-dome-marking-second-official-acquisition)). It is not a comfortable pick. A UK founder cannot trade these venues, may not be able to open a Kalshi API account, and has funded competitors. Those handicaps can be tested in **10 days for under £1,000**. If any one of them is fatal, the fallback is **Veto**, the pre-trade check and decision ledger from earlier research. Veto is the safest and most founder-fit option, but it has a lower ceiling and an unproven willingness to pay for "no". The most likely outcome of either is still failure or a small business. The difference is that the upside now has precedents worth tens of millions, where before it was a £15k trade.

**Evidence labels.** **[Verified]** means a primary source (regulator, statute, SEC filing, official platform document) or several independent reputable sources agree. **[Strong]** means strong evidence: reputable press or law-firm summaries, or consistent secondary sources. **[Anecdotal]** means a single vendor, blog, review, affiliate site or self-reported figure. **[Speculative]** means my inference, scoring or arithmetic. **[Unknown]** means the research could not establish it. Many source pages could only be read as search extracts, and the labels reflect that. Research date: **25 October 2026**. Nothing here is legal or tax advice.

## 1. Executive verdict: the angle is wrong, and the founder's own research proves it

**The 2.8/5 score for listing-announcement trading was a ranking among latency trades, not a business score.** In that ranking it came second of 30 candidates because it reused the most code, not because it made money. Scored against business criteria (revenue ceiling, defensibility, time to first pound), it drops to about **1.8/5** (Section 3). The evidence against it has not changed. A 327-listing study found that Upbit listings had a *negative* average reaction, with 73% of reactions negative, while Binance listings averaged +14.7% [Strong, dated 2019] ([BRL working paper](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Exploring-Market-Reactions-to-Exchange-Listings-of-Cryptocurrencies-BRL-working-paper3.pdf)). For tokens already listed elsewhere, most of the Binance premium is captured before the announcement and reverts after it [Anecdotal] ([The Tie](https://www.thetie.io/insights/what-a-binance-listing-delivers-for-already-listed-tokens)). Public scraper bots were already reacting in about 0.1 seconds in 2021 [Anecdotal] ([GitHub](https://github.com/CyberPunkMetalHead/gateio-crypto-trading-bot-binance-announcements-new-coins)). From 25 October 2027 the UK's crypto market-abuse regime adds a further risk for anyone trading close to listing information [Strong] ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmrvytdhw00e4uom8r2q9i3y7/ps-26-9-market-abuse-regime-for-cryptoassets)). The estimate of **£2k–£15k a year if it works** still stands [Speculative].

**Trading your own edge has a structural ceiling, and every research strand hit it.** Betfair's Expert Fee takes **20% of 52-week gross profit between £25k and £100k and 40% above £100k**, a tax on exactly the success a business would need [Strong] ([Racing Post](https://racingpost.com/news/britain/betfair-exchange-to-introduce-new-commission-system-for-2025-as-premium-charge-is-dropped-a7wbg0v4GCAJ)). Matched betting realistically pays about **£600–£12k a year** and decays as accounts are restricted [Anecdotal] ([UK Tax Calculators](https://www.uktaxcalculators.co.uk/side-hustles/matched-betting/)). Passive liquidity provision on volatile pairs lost money in aggregate: on Uniswap v3 about **$199M of fees against about $260M of impermanent loss**, with 49.5% of LPs behind holding [Verified paper] ([arXiv 2111.09192](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.09192)). The best-evidenced legal own-capital option is a special-situations desk: odd-lot tenders, SPAC trust arbitrage, UK investment-trust tenders and small merger arbitrage. Merger arbitrage historically earned about **4% a year abnormal return** after costs [Verified, academic] ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/merger-spread/)), and the whole desk plausibly makes **£10k–£60k a year on £200k–£500k of capital** [Speculative]. A good side income, but capped by capital rather than by skill.

**Selling tools beats trading by one to three orders of magnitude for small teams.** The comparison that matters is margin as well as revenue. Darwinex, an FCA-regulated broker that turns traders into investable strategies, grew to **€7.0M of revenue with €582K of profit** in 2023 [Strong] ([FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/darwinex-operator-tradeslide-sees-10-revenue-jump-in-2023-to-e7-0m)). RebelBetting, an unlicensed four-person software company, earned roughly 54% EBIT [Strong, undated] ([FT via euro2day](https://www.euro2day.gr/ftcom_en/article-ft-en/1248302/gambling-software-rebelbetting-enables-punters-to.html)). Holding money, executing trades or holding a licence raises the ceiling but crushes the margin. Selling software that judges and records decisions keeps the margin [Speculative, from the two cases].

**The reframe that makes the founder's assets valuable.** Adaptive Quant was built as a player: it scouts trades, sizes them and paper-trades them. Its distinctive parts are a referee's parts. It refuses to fabricate data (`DataUnavailable`). It has a deterministic gate that an LLM cannot override (`risk.py`). It has an adversarial critic (`devils_advocate.py`) and it refuses to draw conclusions below a sample-size threshold (`lesson_min_evidence = 10`, `analytics.evidence_quality`) [Verified, code inspection]. Referees get paid where money is at stake and the rules are ambiguous or mandated. Today the largest and fastest-growing such place is cross-venue prediction markets, where similarly worded contracts can settle differently [Strong] ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/how-contracts-settle/)). The second is the UK crypto regime, which from October 2027 requires even small firms to have "proportionate" market-abuse surveillance [Strong] ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmrvytdhw00e4uom8r2q9i3y7/ps-26-9-market-abuse-regime-for-cryptoassets)). The third, and the most modest, is the trader's own discipline, which is Veto. **UK residency blocks the founder from trading these markets, not from selling software to the world** [Speculative].

**The exit maths shows where the premium sits.** On the seller's 2024 projections, OddsJam's upfront price was about **6.7x EBITDA and 3.1x revenue**, rising to about **13.3x EBITDA and 6.2x revenue** with the full earnout [Speculative arithmetic on Verified deal terms]. The buyer then described itself as a "data and marketing company" and reported 2025 adjusted EBITDA of **$58.0M** [Verified] ([SEC 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001839799/000183979926000076/gamblingcomgrouplimited_an.pdf)). The B2B odds API (OpticOdds) was plausibly what the acquirer paid up for [Speculative].

**Seven patterns separate these businesses from the hobby tools** [Speculative, synthesis].

1. **The buyer has measurable money riding on each decision.** That is why sharp-betting tools charge $99–$799 a month: Unabated's tiers run from $99 to $799 [Anecdotal] ([BetHero](https://betherosports.com/blog/unabated-alternative)). Journals charge $15–$80, and Polymarket whale trackers fall to $9–$20.
2. **The product aggregates something fragmented** across 300 sportsbooks, every congressional disclosure or every card sale.
3. **Timing rode a wave.** Card Ladder, Quiver and Koinly grew in the 2020–21 retail mania, and OddsJam grew with US betting legalisation. The steadier tools (Darwinex, the journals) took 5–15 years to reach single-digit millions [Strong] ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/commerce/2021/collectors-universe-buys-card-ladder-1234647703/); [FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/darwinex-operator-tradeslide-sees-10-revenue-jump-in-2023-to-e7-0m)).
4. **Distribution came from one channel that worked.** For Autopilot it was a founder-run X account ("Pelosi Tracker") [Strong] ([Salon](https://www.salon.com/2025/03/17/pelosi-tracker-shows-us-how-to-trade-stocks-like-politicians/)). Others used affiliates, a free data tier with SEO, or a platform partner, as Bet Angel did as BETDAQ's tool provider [Strong] ([SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/europe/2019/02/11/higher-learning-betdaq-launches-knowledge-portal-betdaqpro-com/)).
5. **They were software only, with no licence.**
6. **A data API sat beside the consumer product** and drove the exit premium.
7. **The exit was to a vertical consolidator**: Gambling.com, Collectors or Lightspeed, never an IPO.

**The failure modes are equally consistent.** The first is a trust breach. At 3Commas, leaked API keys produced verified losses of **$27.3M for 86 users** [Strong] ([ForkLog/HAPI](https://forklog.com/en/news/api-key-leaks-and-exchange-inaction-a-hapi-analysis-of-the-3commas-incident)). The second is incumbent legal attack: Entain sued OddsMonkey and Outplayed in the High Court [Strong] ([iGaming Next](https://next.io/?p=135928)). The third is advertising regulation, with ASA rulings against matched-betting promotion [Verified] ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/dotnetpages-ltd-a18-474571.html)). The fourth is dependence on a platform owner. Valve's game changes have repriced the CS2 skin market by billions overnight [Verified] ([Dust2.us](https://www.dust2.us/news/66949/cs2-skins-market-craters-17-billion-after-october-22nd-update-throws-market-into-chaos)).

**The honest base case is Collectr's, not OddsJam's**: a long flat stretch at five-figure ARR before a channel clicks [Strong] ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/how-collectr-bootstrapped-a-trading-card-hobby-into-an-eight-figure-business/)).

## 4. The top three, stress-tested

### 4.1 Parity: the venues will never map their contracts to their rivals', which leaves room for a neutral referee

**The opportunity.** Prediction markets are the one live demand wave in this space. Combined Kalshi and Polymarket monthly volume went from under $5B in September 2025 [Anecdotal] ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/09/08/uk-fca-reviews-prediction-markets-as-kalshi-polymarket-gain-ground/)) to a record of about **$50.6B in July 2026** [Strong] ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/web3/2026-08-03-kalshi-polymarket-volume-july-410382)). Robinhood booked **$156M of event-contract revenue in Q2 2026** [Strong] ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/prediction-markets-take-center-stage-in-latest-quarterly-earnings.html)). The venue count has exploded to more than ten meaningful venues [Strong for the launches] ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-18-crypto-com-novig-sue-missouri-over-sports-event-contracts-kalshi-polymarket-robinhood-hold-off-418866)): Polymarket international and US, Kalshi, Robinhood, Coinbase, DraftKings, FanDuel, Crypto.com, Novig, Hyperliquid HIP-4, Opinion, Pascal, plus the UK exchanges. Fragmentation is OddsJam's pattern (aggregating 300 books), replayed in a new market. The buyers are professional. DraftKings' CEO estimated that **80–90% of prediction-market volume comes from syndicates and institutional traders** [Strong, executive estimate] ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/prediction-markets-take-center-stage-in-latest-quarterly-earnings.html)). Professionals pay for data, and the retail majority (70–84% of wallets lose) churns.

**The specific pain.** Venues settle differently. Kalshi's internal team decides outcomes and offers no binding trader dispute. On Polymarket anyone can dispute by posting a **$750 USDC bond**, which escalates to a token vote [Strong] ([DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/how-contracts-settle/)). Similarly worded markets on different venues can therefore settle differently. Wording differences and fees routinely erase the 1–5% cross-venue edges that scanners advertise [Strong for existence] ([Apify verifier](https://apify.com/redfoxxie/polymarket-kalshi-arbitrage-verifier); [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/arbitrage/kalshi)). Earlier research found **no systematic dataset of how often "equivalent" Polymarket and Kalshi markets resolve differently** [Unknown]. That gap is the product. The structural argument is that **no venue has an incentive to map its contracts to a rival's, and the institutional vendors are priced for institutions**, so a neutral, cheap and honest mapping layer has room [Speculative].

**The money and the exits are real.** Builder-attributed volume on Polymarket's Builders Program reportedly rose from about $100M in November 2025 to more than $600M in March 2026 [Anecdotal] ([BotForKalshi](https://www.botforkalshi.com/blog/polymarket-builder-program-guide)). Builders already pay for data. Apify sells Polymarket whale data at **$60 per 1,000 calls** [Anecdotal] ([Apify](https://apify.com/apimie/polymarket-whales-pro)), and Polywhaler's API tier costs $99/mo or $599/yr [Anecdotal] ([PredictionAITools](https://predictionaitools.com/tools/polywhaler)). Consolidators are buying: **Polymarket acquired Dome**, a YC-backed unified prediction-market API, in February 2026 [Verified] ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/390546/polymarket-buys-fresh-prediction-market-api-startup-dome-marking-second-official-acquisition)), and Unusual Whales absorbed Hashdive [Anecdotal] ([GamingToday](https://www.gamingtoday.com/prediction-markets/calculators/hashdive/)). Dome now belongs to one venue, which plausibly makes builders who also use Kalshi want a neutral alternative [Speculative].

**The case against is serious, and every item can be tested.** Five problems stand out.

1. **Funded competition.** River Markets raised an **$8.5M seed** for institutional cross-platform trading software and risk tools [Strong] ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/11/exclusive-river-markets-seed-round-prediction-market-trading-tools-institutions/)). Furnace raised **$3.5M** to be a "common reference layer" for traders, which is the closest direct threat [Strong] ([EGR Intel](https://www.egr.global/intel/news/prediction-markets-infrastructure-startup-secures-3-5m-in-funding/)). Elastics raised $2M for prediction-market AI agents [Strong] ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/05/06/elastics-raises-2m-pre-seed-for-ai-prediction-market-agents/)). Cross-venue spread tools also already exist as MCP tools [Anecdotal] ([PolicyLayer](https://policylayer.com/tools/io-github-pipeworx-io-f1/polymarket-kalshi-spread)).
2. **Data access for a UK resident.** Polymarket's Gamma, Data and CLOB read endpoints need **no authentication** [Verified] ([Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/authentication)). Kalshi API access reportedly **requires a KYC'd account** [Anecdotal] ([Allium](https://allium.so/blog/kalshi-api-what-kyc-and-fees-mean-for-access/)), and Kalshi lists the UK as restricted [Strong] ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/prediction-markets/polymarket-review/)). If no public or licensed route to Kalshi data exists, the product loses the larger venue, since Kalshi is roughly 3x Polymarket by volume.
3. **Redistribution terms.** Whether Polymarket's or Kalshi's terms allow commercial redistribution of market data is **Unknown**. It matters because the venues sell exclusive media data deals: Kalshi with CNBC and Polymarket with Dow Jones [Verified/Strong] ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/381415/kalshi-exclusive-cnbc-deal-prediction-markets-surge-mainstream-media); [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/polymarket-deal-with-dow-jones-brings-data-to-wsj-marketwatch-barrons/)). Derived data (equivalence maps, rule diffs, risk scores) is arguably different from raw prices, but that is a legal question [Speculative].
4. **Gambling-adjacency from the UK.** The Gambling Commission says unlicensed prediction operators should not target or transact with consumers in Great Britain [Strong] ([InterGame](https://www.intergameonline.com/igaming/news/prediction-markets-would-likely-be-classed-as-gambling-in-gb)). No source addresses analytics vendors [Unknown].
5. **Ecosystem risk.** US states keep narrowing sports and political contracts. Washington has ordered Kalshi to stop sports, election, politics and several other categories, and the Sixth Circuit has held that Kalshi's sports contracts are not swaps [Verified/Strong] ([WA AG](https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/judge-orders-kalshi-cease-numerous-washington-operations); [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/09/25/kalshi-sixth-circuit-sides-oh-tn/)).

The founder cannot use the product with real money, which removes the Bet Angel advantage of a founder who trades in public.

**Verdict.** This option has the highest ceiling and the best pattern match. It also has the most UK-specific handicaps, and four of them can be settled cheaply in the first ten days. That combination makes it the right *first* bet: the potential prize is large, and finding out whether it is open is cheap. Section 5 sets out the full plan.

### 4.2 Veto: the best-fitting product, still waiting for proof that anyone pays for "no"

**The case for.** Earlier research scored Veto highest of eight concepts (42 out of 60). It is a pre-trade check and decision ledger: it checks a human trader's proposed trade against their own rules and verified history, returns CLEAR, CAUTION or STAND DOWN, and counts the trades they skipped. It reuses roughly 70% of Adaptive Quant, never executes or advises, and sits at the bottom of the UK regulatory risk ladder. In the UK, advising on and managing unbacked cryptoassets were not made regulated activities, and a non-executing tool stays clear of "arranging" under PERG 18 [Strong] ([Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/drawing-the-line-navigating-the-fcas-new-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance-102mvlt); [FCA CP26/36](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp26-36-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance)). The new benchmark research adds real support. Trading journals are a durable, mostly bootstrapped and profitable category: Tradervue since 2011, TraderSync since 2014, Edgewonk at an estimated ~$5.5M, and TradeZella calling itself "highly profitable" [Anecdotal] ([Datanyze](https://www.datanyze.com/companies/edgewonk/415056718); [TradeZella](https://www.tradezella.com/careers)). Discipline tools do sell, and the category reaches the target band. The founder can also use it daily on their own crypto-spot trading.

**The case against.** Journals are marketed as a **path to profitability, not as loss avoidance**, and no business marketed explicitly as "lose less" was found at scale [Anecdotal/Unknown]. Journal pricing is commoditising at $15–$80 a month, so £300k ARR needs about **1,000 subscribers at £25**, a consumer-marketing grind for one person [Speculative]. There is no demand wave behind it. Behaviour change is doubtful: in Brazil, 97% of people who day-traded for 300+ days lost money, with "no evidence of learning" [Verified] ([FGV](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full)). The core gate can be cloned in a weekend, and TradeZella or TraderSync could ship a pre-trade check within a quarter. Affiliate-led growth collides with FG24/1 when affiliates induce crypto investment [Strong] ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation)).

**Verdict.** Veto is the **fallback**. It is the option most likely to produce *some* revenue and the least likely to produce a £5m business. Its fatal unknown, whether people will pay to be told "no", takes 30–90 days of live users to answer, against Parity's ten days. That timing difference is why Veto goes second. If Veto is activated, the new evidence says to reposition it as "protect your edge": a journal with a pre-trade gate, sold on profitability and process, not as a loss-prevention product.

### 4.3 Small-firm crypto surveillance: the most literal "too small for big vendors" niche, sold to the wrong kind of buyer for a solo founder

**The case for.** The timeline is fixed. The FCA's crypto authorisation gateway opened on **30 September 2026**, firms should apply by **28 February 2027**, the regime goes live on **25 October 2027**, and existing MLR registrations do not convert [Strong] ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/fca-s-cryptoasset-authorisation-gateway-now-open); [Farrer & Co](https://www.farrer.co.uk/news-and-insights/high-stakes-uk-cryptoasset-regulation/)). PS26/9 requires trading platforms and intermediaries to have "effective, proportionate" systems to prevent, detect and disrupt market abuse. Heavier on-chain monitoring applies only to large platforms with more than **£10m** of average annual revenue, and smaller firms are explicitly expected to use proportionate measures, which may include regtech [Strong] ([Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmrvytdhw00e4uom8r2q9i3y7/ps-26-9-market-abuse-regime-for-cryptoassets)). Enterprise vendors (Solidus Labs, Eventus, Chainalysis, Elliptic) all sell on quote [Unknown pricing], which leaves the long tail of small firms underserved, the literal meaning of the founder's brief [Speculative]. Demand is mandated, B2B contracts are sticky, and UK residency is an asset here. The founder's own listing-announcement research turns from a trade into a detection rule: pre-announcement trading in tokens about to be admitted is exactly what the new admissions and market-abuse rules police [Speculative] ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/04/crypto-admissions-disclosures-and-market-abuse-regime)). A deterministic engine an LLM cannot override is what a compliance officer wants: the LLM drafts the alert narrative and a human closes the case. At an indicative **£1k–£4k a month**, twenty clients is a £240k–£960k business [Speculative pricing].

**The case against.** Nobody knows **how many UK firms will apply or how many sit below the £10m threshold** [Unknown]. The notes describe the registered population as historically small. Compliance buyers are risk-averse and prefer established vendors. A solo vendor for a regulatory-critical function raises outsourcing and resilience questions, and the founder has no evident compliance credibility [Speculative]. Applicants are choosing their systems *now*, while the gateway is open, so a founder who would ship an MVP in Q1 2027 is late for the application wave and early only for go-live [Speculative]. Incumbents are already circling, with StarCompliance writing to this market [Anecdotal] ([StarCompliance](https://www.starcompliance.com/the-uks-2025-crypto-regulatory-overhaul/)). Properly built surveillance also needs ingestion of client order data, case management, a suspicious transaction and order report (STOR) workflow and security attestations. That is a much bigger build than a dashboard.

**Verdict.** Not recommended for a solo founder. It becomes the primary **only if** two things are both true. First, a count of the FCA register and applicant pipeline shows at least 30 addressable small firms. Second, the founder can sign a compliance consultancy or an experienced former MLRO as a channel partner or co-founder before building. Without both, it is a sales problem disguised as a software opportunity.

## 5. The recommendation: Parity, a settlement-risk data layer for cross-venue prediction-market builders

**One-sentence offer.** Parity tells prediction-market builders and cross-venue traders which contracts on different venues are genuinely the same bet, how their settlement rules differ, how likely they are to settle differently, and what the cross-venue spread is at a given size after fees. It is delivered as an API, an MCP server and a dashboard, and it never places an order [Speculative design].

### Exact customer

The first paying customer is a **non-UK developer or small team (1–10 people) that runs a prediction-market bot, trading terminal, AI-agent product, analytics site or small syndicate touching at least two venues, typically Polymarket and Kalshi**. Today they maintain a hand-built matching spreadsheet or script and have lost money, or fear losing it, on a "hedge" that settled one way on one venue and the other way on the other. They are technical, buy with a credit card, and already pay $50–$600 a month for data or infrastructure [Speculative persona, grounded in the builder, API-pricing and settlement evidence in Section 4.1]. The second customer is the **serious cross-venue trader or syndicate analyst outside the UK** who wants the same information in a dashboard with alerts. UK users are excluded at launch. A UK module for Matchbook Predictions, Betfair Predicts, Smarkets and IG is added only when those venues' APIs and data terms allow; that is old option (d), turned into a feature [Strong for the venues' status] ([European Gaming](https://europeangaming.eu/portal/latest-news/2026/06/11/206782/one-uk-licence-two-prediction-brands-matchbooks-world-cup-play/); [SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/sportsbook/2026/10/22/ig-group-uk-predictions-launch/)).

### Offer

The core is a **canonical cross-venue event map**: one ID per real-world question, linked to each venue's contract. Every link carries a **settlement rule diff** covering source, timestamp, edge cases, dispute mechanism and clarifications, plus a **Settlement Divergence Risk score**. The score is computed by deterministic checks over LLM-extracted rule fields, and the LLM cannot publish a mapping the validator rejects. The second layer is the **executable spread**: cross-venue price at a chosen size after each venue's fee formula. Kalshi's taker fee peaks near 50¢ at about 1.75¢ per contract, which erases most naive edges [Verified] ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)). The third is a **resolution watch**: webhooks when rules are edited, clarifications are issued or a dispute bond is posted. The fourth, and the long-term moat, is the **resolution-history dataset**: every mapped pair's actual outcomes, with a published calibration curve of the risk score that is shown only once the sample-size gate is met. That is how Parity becomes the cited authority, as Pricempire became for CS2 prices [Strong for the Pricempire precedent] ([Dust2.us](https://www.dust2.us/news/66949/cs2-skins-market-craters-17-billion-after-october-22nd-update-throws-market-into-chaos)).

### Pricing (test ranges, priced in USD because customers are outside the UK)

### Honest expected outcome

The subjective odds, all [Speculative], run as follows. Roughly an even chance of passing the day-10 gates. About a one-in-four chance of reaching £3k MRR by day 90 if the gates pass. Roughly **one in ten of reaching £300k ARR within three years**. If it works, a "good" outcome is **£300k–£1m ARR with one to three people by 2028**. An exceptional outcome is £2m–£5m, with a strategic exit to a venue, a data consolidator or a betting-data group. Prediction markets show the same consolidation pattern so far: Polymarket bought Dome and Unusual Whales absorbed Hashdive. The most likely single outcome is a kill at day 10 or day 30, at a cost of a few weeks and under £1,000. That still compares favourably with every own-capital option, whose most likely outcome was also £0 and whose success case was capped at about £15k.

### The fallback: Veto, and exactly when to switch

If any day-10 or day-30 gate fails, start **Veto** immediately. The customer is a self-directed crypto-spot trader on Binance, Coinbase or Kraken, two or more years in, making 5–50 trades a week and already paying for TradingView or a journal. Price it at **£12–£15 entry, £25–£29 core and £49–£59 pro** a month, with read-only sync only. Run it as the 30-day validation with pre-registered kill criteria designed in earlier research, repositioned as "protect your edge" (Section 4.2). Most of Parity's first 30 days of work (async recorders, the deterministic validator, metering and billing) carries over.

## 7. Legal notes (research, not legal advice)

**UK gambling law is the first question for Parity.** The Gambling Commission's February 2026 position is that current prediction-market products appear to fall within the "betting intermediary" definition, that unlicensed operators "should take steps to ensure they are not targeting or transacting with consumers in Great Britain", and that operating without a licence is a criminal offence [Strong] ([InterGame](https://www.intergameonline.com/igaming/news/prediction-markets-would-likely-be-classed-as-gambling-in-gb)). Polymarket is close-only or blocked for the UK, and Kalshi lists the UK as restricted [Strong] ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/prediction-markets/polymarket-review/)). The Dutch regulator took action against Polymarket's operator over ineffective geoblocking [Strong] ([FocusGN](https://focusgn.com/gambling-commission-warns-prediction-markets-not-to-target-the-uk)). Read-only analytics software that neither accepts bets nor matches bettors does not obviously fit "betting intermediary", but **no source rules on analytics vendors** [Speculative/Unknown]. The defensible posture is narrow. Sell B2B and to non-UK users only. Geo-exclude GB at sign-up and in advertising. Carry no affiliate or referral links to unlicensed venues. Never route, sign or relay orders. Polymarket's own developer documents tell builders to verify user geolocation *before placing orders*, which Parity never does [Anecdotal] ([Polymarket geoblock docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock.md)). Confirm all of this with a UK solicitor before day 10.

**The FCA view of financial event contracts reinforces the GB exclusion.** The FCA treats the financial prediction products it has examined as **binary options**, and the 2019 retail ban stands. Sports and political contracts fall to the Gambling Commission [Strong] ([The Paypers](https://thepaypers.com/crypto-web3-and-cbdc/news/fca-reportedly-confirms-talks-as-prediction-markets-push-back-on-gambling-label)). Marketing tools for financial event contracts to UK persons could raise financial-promotion questions under s21 FSMA [Speculative]. That is another reason the UK module should cover only UK-licensed venues.

**US exposure sits with the venues, so far.** State actions have targeted operators: injunctions and orders in Massachusetts, Michigan and Washington, a New York AG petition, and Missouri cease-and-desist letters [Verified] ([VitalLaw](https://www.vitallaw.com/news/enforcement-mass-cmmw-massachusetts-court-bars-kalshi-from-offering-sports-event-contracts-without-state-license/sld019d9a03c2d0e148a8abbe7a0d3e69cd0a); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-18-crypto-com-novig-sue-missouri-over-sports-event-contracts-kalshi-polymarket-robinhood-hold-off-418866)). No action against a data vendor was found [Unknown]. Earlier research noted that US advisory rules exempt impersonal, standardised publications and software that does not direct accounts: the Lowe publisher exclusion and CFTC Rule 4.14(a)(9) [Strong] ([Lowe v. SEC](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/472-u-s-181-604990426)). Parity's outputs should therefore stay impersonal: facts about contracts, never "take this trade". A US lawyer should confirm before US marketing scales.

**Data terms are a contract-law risk, not a regulatory one.** Commercial redistribution rights for Polymarket and Kalshi data are **Unknown**, and so are Betfair's for any use of Betfair prices as a reference. Betfair does not allow read-only use of its Live key [Strong] ([Betfair Application Keys docs](https://betfair-developer-docs.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/1smk3cen4v3lu3yomq5qye0ni/pages/2687105/Application+Keys)), so a commercial reference feed probably needs a vendor agreement [Speculative]. Design Parity so that it sells *derived* facts (maps, rule diffs, risk scores, computed spreads) and links to venues for raw prices [Speculative].

**For the fallback, Veto.** Advising on and managing unbacked cryptoassets are not regulated activities under the UK regime. Giving users a way to place orders is likely to be "arranging" under PERG 18, with a narrow technical-interface exclusion under consultation until 18 November 2026 [Strong] ([FCA CP26/36](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp26-36-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance)). For shares and CFDs, software that generates signals on particular investments is generally advice unless the user controls the parameters [Strong] ([PERG 8.30](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/8/30.html)). Veto stays safe by never executing and only checking user-proposed trades against the user's own rules. Its marketing must never claim returns, and affiliates who induce crypto investment fall under FG24/1 [Strong] ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/10/social-media-meets-financial-regulation)). Hold read-only keys only, which is the 3Commas lesson [Strong] ([ForkLog/HAPI](https://forklog.com/en/news/api-key-leaks-and-exchange-inaction-a-hapi-analysis-of-the-3commas-incident)).

**For the options not chosen.** Selling "volume support" is prosecuted as wash trading: Gotbit's founder was sentenced and about $23M was forfeited [Verified] ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/cryptocurrency-financial-services-firm-gotbit-and-founder-sentenced-market-manipulation)). Token market making needs FCA authorisation from October 2027. Retail crypto derivatives remain banned for UK residents [Verified] ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-proposes-ban-sale-crypto-derivatives-retail-consumers)). Betting profits are generally untaxed for individuals, but a software company's revenue is ordinary trading income [Strong] ([UK Tax Calculators](https://www.uktaxcalculators.co.uk/side-hustles/matched-betting/)).

## Conclusion

The search went wrong because it treated the founder as a player looking for a table. Every legal table open to a UK resident turned out to cap winnings at tens of thousands of pounds. Bots are faster than the founder, fees cap success, and the venues close edges as soon as they see them. The founder's distinctive work was never a signal. It was the machinery that stops a signal from lying: no fabricated data, deterministic gates, adversarial review and minimum sample sizes. That machinery is worth more sold than used. The same UK rules that block the founder from trading prediction markets and crypto derivatives are what make a neutral, careful referee valuable, both to builders outside the UK and, later, to UK-licensed venues and regulated firms.

"Being early", the instinct behind the listing-announcement idea, survives in a better form. The lesson of 0x8dxd and of OddsJam is the same: arrive while a new market is fragmented and its plumbing is immature. For a trader, that window closes in months, when the venue adds fees. For a data provider it can last years, because fragmentation is the product. The right next step is not a better bot or a longer recorder run. It is ten days of reading terms, one hour with a solicitor and twenty conversations with builders, with Veto ready as the fallback if the answer is no.
