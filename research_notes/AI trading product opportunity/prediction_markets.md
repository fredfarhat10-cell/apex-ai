# Prediction-Market Bots, AI Agents and Tooling (Polymarket / Kalshi) — late-2026 notes, UK builder perspective

Research date: 2026-10-25. Evidence labels: **[Verified]** = primary source (official docs, regulator, GitHub API, peer-reviewed or arXiv paper) read directly or quoted in search; **[Strong]** = several independent credible secondary sources agree; **[Anecdotal]** = single secondary or vendor or blog source; **[Speculative]** = forecast or opinion; **[Unknown]** = could not establish. Caveats: the docs.polymarket.com and bitquery.io hosts did not resolve from this environment (DNS failure), so figures from those sites come from search-engine extracts of the pages, not full reads. ## 1. What bots, trackers, arbitrage tools and AI agents exist? Pricing and traction

### Takeaway
The tooling ecosystem is large but shallow. There are hundreds of open-source bots and agents, a few freemium whale trackers (about $29/mo tier), copy-trading front-ends and MCP or agent-skill wrappers. Polymarket's own flagship `agents` repo is now **archived**. Traction figures (stars, MAU, revenue) are mostly unaudited, and no tooling vendor has disclosed audited revenue.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** `Polymarket/agents` ("Trade autonomously on Polymarket using AI Agents", Python, created Jul 2024) has about 3.8k stars and 826 forks, and is **archived** (read-only) per the GitHub API as of Oct 2026. — [GitHub: Polymarket/agents](https://github.com/Polymarket/agents)
- **[Verified]** Polymarket now publishes `Polymarket/agent-skills` (created Feb 2026, about 193 stars). This shows a pivot from a reference agent to "skills" for general-purpose LLM agents (Claude Code and similar). — [GitHub: Polymarket/agent-skills](https://github.com/Polymarket/agent-skills)
- **[Verified]** GitHub search for "polymarket agents" returns about 662 repos. Examples: an MCP server letting Claude or ChatGPT fetch odds and execute trades (about 209 stars), an ElizaOS Polymarket agent "pamela", "phil" (a self-rewriting short-term-market trader, paper-trades with small capped real stakes, about 237 stars, created Jul 2026), and "predict-raven" (claims to be "live on Polymarket"). The curated list `Awesome-Prediction-Market-Tools` has about 783 stars and covers AI agents, analytics, copy trading and alerting across Kalshi and Polymarket. Star counts measure attention, not profitability. — [GitHub search results](https://github.com/aarora4/Awesome-Prediction-Market-Tools), [ai-polymarket-agent](https://github.com/kaktusesquire6rmu/ai-polymarket-agent), [phil](https://github.com/bennyjo/phil), [predict-raven](https://github.com/Alchemist-X/predict-raven), [pamela](https://github.com/theSchein/pamela)

This shows low-code builders are commoditising basic bot plumbing. — [n8n community](https://community.n8n.io/t/looking-for-beta-testers-polymarket-prediction-market-node-with-trading-whale-tracking-and-ai-agent-support/286077?tl=es)

- **[Verified]** Polymarket runs a **Maker Rebates Program** that pays market makers daily in USDC, funded by taker fees. ### Inferences
- **[Speculative]** Basic bot infrastructure (API wrappers, MCP servers, whale alerts, copy trading) is crowded and near-free. Polymarket itself ships agent skills, which squeezes any product whose value is "connect an LLM to Polymarket".
- **[Speculative]** The archiving of `Polymarket/agents` hints that Polymarket stopped treating "LLM agent picks bets" as a flagship use case. It may equally reflect the move to the v2 CLOB clients and agent-skills format. I could not confirm a stated reason.
- **[Speculative]** Copy trading has a structural flaw: copied whales are often market makers or hedgers whose individual fills are not directional signals. Copying also adds latency and slippage.

### Gaps
- **[Unknown]** Paying-user numbers, revenue and churn for PolyTrack, Polywhaler, TraderWin or any copy-trading service. None publish them.
- **[Unknown]** Verified live P&L for any open-source AI agent repo. Repos claim "live" status but show no audited track record.
- I found no Kalshi-specific commercial bot vendor with credible traction data.

## 2. Evidence of profitability — what really works vs viral myths

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence says (a) arbitrage profits are real but modest at platform scale (about $40M over 12 months) and highly concentrated, (b) about 70–88% of wallets lose money, and (c) bots dominate trade counts but most individual bots lose. Maker-side bots do far better than taker bots. Latency arbitrage on short-term crypto markets was the real money-maker in 2025, and Polymarket has since tried to fee it away.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer and Suarez-Tangil, "Unravelling the Probabilistic Forest: Arbitrage in Prediction Markets" (arXiv 2508.03474, Aug 2025; AFT 2025, LIPIcs vol. 354). The paper uses on-chain order-book data and separates "Market Rebalancing Arbitrage" (intra-market, e.g. YES+NO or the sum of outcomes ≠ $1) from "Combinatorial Arbitrage" (across related markets). It estimates about **$40M realised arbitrage profit** across both types in the measurement window. — [arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03474v1), [Dagstuhl AFT 2025](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol354-aft2025/html/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27.html)
- **[Strong]** Secondary write-ups give the window as **April 2024 – April 2025**, about 86M bets, with the **top 10 wallets capturing about 80%** of arbitrage profits. — [search summary of IMDEA study via webcoda](https://ai-checker.webcoda.com.au/articles/ai-bots-polymarket-trading-profits-40-million-2026), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/arbitrage-bots-dominate-polymarket-millions-100000888.html). The 80% concentration figure comes from secondary sources; I did not confirm it in the paper text.
- **[Strong]** Bitquery investigation (Sep 2025 – Aug 2026): "bots" (wallets trading around the clock with short inter-order gaps) are about 1 in 200 wallets but **63% of trades, 28% of dollar volume and 37% of taker fees**. By Aug 2026 almost half of matched dollar volume had a machine on at least one side, up from a quarter in Sep 2025. — [Bitquery: How much of Polymarket is bots](https://bitquery.io/investigations/how-much-of-polymarket-is-bots). I read the search extract only; the site was unreachable for a full read.
- **[Anecdotal]** Bloomberg reportedly found about 5% of bot-like wallets generate about 75% of volume. CNBC reportedly found bots are more than 80% of volume in markets under $10k. Both are cited secondhand only. — [via webcoda/search summaries](https://ai-checker.webcoda.com.au/articles/ai-bots-polymarket-trading-profits-40-million-2026)
- **[Anecdotal]** Vendor study tracking about 2,500 bots for 28 days on BTC Up/Down markets: only **32% profitable after fees and rebates**, and the **median bot lost $59**. **49% of maker-only bots were profitable vs 26% of taker-only bots.** 4.7% of wallets trading around the clock did 70% of volume. This is a vendor with a conflict of interest, but the direction matches market-microstructure theory. — [polymtradebot: Do Polymarket bots work?](https://polymtradebot.com/blog/do-polymarket-bots-work)
- **[Strong]** Share of all traders who profit:
  - Sergeenkov (Apr 2026): about **84% of 2.5M wallets in the red**, only 2% above $1k cumulative profit, 840 wallets (0.033%) above $100k. He attributes the higher loss share to accounting for wallet splits and merges. — [The Defiant, Apr 2026](https://thedefiant.io/news/research-and-opinion/polymarket-profitability-report-april-2026), [DigitalToday](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/49006/polymarket-traders-84-percent-lose-money-in-prediction-market)
  - DeFi Oasis (Dec 2025): about 70% of 1.7M addresses have realised losses, and fewer than 0.04% of addresses captured more than 70% of $3.7B total realised profit. Most losers lose less than $1k (63.5% of participants). — [DeFi Rate](https://defirate.com/news/polymarkets-loss-stats-hide-a-different-reality/)
  - Reichenbach and Walter (2025): about 70% losing. Layerhub: 12.7% profitable (21,730 of 171,113 wallets). Dune (via Decrypt): 86% negative realised P&L. — [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/on-chain-analysis-reveals-profitability-of-polymarket-wallets/amp), [Cryptometer](https://www.cryptometer.io/news/only-12-7-of-polymarket-users-report-profits/), [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/290625/most-polymarket-traders-have-lost-money?amp=1)
- **[Anecdotal]** Solidus Labs data cited by CoinDesk: 0.55% of profitable maker wallets captured half of all gains in politics markets. I saw this only via a search summary. — [search summary](https://ai-checker.webcoda.com.au/articles/ai-bots-polymarket-trading-profits-40-million-2026)
- **[Strong]** The viral "**$313 → about $438k in a month**" wallet (0x8dxd, Dec 2025): about 6,615 predictions at a reported 98% win rate. Widely described as **latency arbitrage** on 15-minute BTC Up/Down markets, reacting to Binance/Coinbase spot moves before Polymarket quotes updated, **not directional prediction**. Reported totals vary ($414k / $438k / $492k). The "Claude-powered" framing comes mainly from promotional sites that sell bots. — [Finbold, 6 Jan 2026](https://finbold.com/trading-bot-turns-313-into-438000-on-polymarket-in-a-month/), [Bitget News](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605137629)
- **[Strong]** Polymarket responded with **dynamic taker fees on 15-minute crypto markets** (Jan 2026), explicitly to curb latency arbitrage. Fees peak near a 50¢ price, about 1.5% effective at $0.45 per the docs table, and about 3% of trade value at $0.50 per Cointelegraph's estimate. — [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/), [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/polymarket-quietly-adds-taker-fees-15-minute-crypto-markets), [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384461/polymarket-adds-taker-fees-to-15-minute-crypto-markets-to-fund-liquidity-rebates)
- **[Anecdotal]** A vendor claims Polymarket stopped settling short-term crypto Up/Down markets on a single price snapshot from 7 Aug 2026, which would close another latency or snapshot edge. Unconfirmed. — [search summary of vendor claims](https://polymtradebot.com/)
- **[Anecdotal]** Cross-venue (Polymarket vs Kalshi) arbitrage: gross gaps of about 1–5¢ on flagship markets, often 0.5–1.5¢ net of fees. The main risk is **resolution-rule mismatch** (different sources or timestamps; UMA optimistic-oracle disputes), which can turn a "hedge" into a one-way loss. Capital also sits idle because it cannot move between venues within the trade window. There is no peer-reviewed measurement. — [pm.wiki Polymarket–Kalshi arbitrage](https://pm.wiki/uk/learn/polymarket-kalshi-arbitrage), [tech-insider](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/prediction-market-arbitrage/), [simplefunctions cross-venue convergence](https://simplefunctions.dev/concepts/cross-venue-convergence)
- **[Anecdotal]** Many Medium and dev.to posts ("Claude AI trading bots making hundreds of thousands", "make $500/day copy trading") show survivorship-biased winners and frequently promote paid bots or referral links. — [Medium example](https://medium.com/@weare1010/claude-ai-trading-bots-are-making-hundreds-of-thousands-on-polymarket-2840efb9f2cd), [dev.to example](https://dev.to/andrew-ooo/how-ai-trading-bots-are-making-millions-on-polymarket-l5g)

### Inferences
- **[Strong inference]** Real, repeatable edges, ranked: (1) **subsidised market making** (maker rebates, zero maker fees, maker bots profitable roughly 2x as often as taker bots); (2) **latency/oracle arbitrage on short-dated crypto markets**, which was very profitable in 2025 and is now deliberately taxed; (3) **intra-market rebalancing / combinatorial arbitrage**, which is real but about $40M per year platform-wide and about 80% captured by about 10 wallets, so a new entrant competes with entrenched low-latency players for crumbs; (4) **insider information**, which "works" and is illegal (see section 5).
- **[Speculative]** "LLM predicts outcomes better than market" as a standalone retail strategy has no audited public P&L evidence (see section 3). It is the most-marketed and least-evidenced strategy.
- **[Speculative]** $40M per year of arb profit vs multi-billion annual volume implies arbitrage is a small, competitive niche, not a mass-market product opportunity.

### Gaps
- No independently audited P&L for any named commercial bot product.
- No academic measurement of market-maker profitability on Polymarket or Kalshi after 2026 rebates.
- No systematic data on how often Polymarket and Kalshi resolve "equivalent" markets differently.
- Bloomberg and CNBC bot-volume analyses could not be read in the original.

## 3. AI forecasting evidence — LLMs vs superforecasters vs markets

### Takeaway
As of mid/late 2026, the best LLM systems are **close to, but on most measures still behind, top human forecasters**, and **roughly at par with or slightly behind liquid markets**. Projected parity dates cluster around late 2026. Rankings change depending on whether you score accuracy, calibration or simulated trading return. Good forecasting does not automatically mean profit after fees and spreads.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** ForecastBench (Forecasting Research Institute), Oct 2025 launch post: GPT-4.5 Brier **0.101** vs superforecasters **0.081** (lower is better). Projected LLM–superforecaster parity "late 2026" with a wide interval to Jan 2028. — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-llm-forecasting-model-forecastbench-benchmark)
- **[Verified]** FRI follow-up ("LLMs are closing the gap"): superforecasters lead SOTA models by **0.017 Brier**, about one year of LLM progress. Grok 4.20 preview (Oct 2025) scored **0.102**. Extrapolated parity **Nov 2026** (95% CI Jan 2026 – Nov 2027), with market-question parity projected for Aug 2026. — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human)
- **[Anecdotal]** On a 76-question market subset, one summary reports a superforecaster median of 0.0740, market consensus 0.0965 and public median 0.1035. This suggests superforecasters beat market prices on that subset. It is a secondary summary. — [Longterm Wiki ForecastBench](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/responses/epistemic-tools/tools/forecastbench)
- **[Anecdotal]** The "AIA Forecaster" paper (Nov 2025) claims superforecaster-level ForecastBench performance via agentic search, supervisor aggregation and extremisation (Platt scaling). Not independently replicated in what I found. — [Emergent Mind ForecastBench topic](https://emergentmind.com/topics/forecastbench)
- **[Verified]** Metaculus FutureEval Spring 2026 (Jan–Apr 2026, 173 bots, 297 scored questions): Pros beat bots **but not statistically significantly**. 9 of 10 individual Pros beat every individual bot. The top 10 bots were statistically indistinguishable from one another, and 7 of the top 10 bot makers were hobbyists. A $50k Fall 2026 tournament is under way. — [LessWrong: FutureEval spring results](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wZBbDqzfBjYG58CxK/futureeval-spring-results-pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is), [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/iJ3qBDwpWCfNy9ZGd/pros-beat-bots-but-the-gap-is-nearly-gone), [Fall 2026 announcement](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/hrJrsgMCwsdBuAqgQ/announcing-fall-2026-futureeval-bot-tournament)
- **[Verified]** Earlier Metaculus Q1 (2025) result: "Pro forecasters crush bots". This shows how fast the gap narrowed between 2025 and 2026. — [LessWrong Q1 results](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rDy5z8ZEtMrEGnfBd/q1-ai-benchmark-results-pro-forecasters-crush-bots)
- **[Verified]** Prophet Arena ("LLM-as-a-Prophet", arXiv 2510.17638, ICLR 2026) benchmarks LLMs on live Kalshi-type events with Brier, calibration and simulated market return. v1 abstract: "small calibration errors… promising market returns". The paper also finds frontier-model weaknesses: inaccurate event recall, misunderstanding data sources and **slower information aggregation than markets as resolution nears**. Another ICLR abstract version says "many LLMs struggle to reach parity with market forecasts". — [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17638v1), [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10009102)
- **[Anecdotal]** A secondary summary of Prophet Arena reports GPT-5 Brier 0.184 / ECE 0.042 / average return 0.943 vs market baseline 0.187 / 0.069 / 0.899. This implies **both lose money** (return below 1.0), with GPT-5 losing slightly less. Model rankings changed by metric. — [Liner review](https://liner.com/ko/review/llmasaprophet-understanding-predictive-intelligence-with-prophet-arena)
- **[Anecdotal]** A May 2026 synthesis ("AI forecasting in 2026: what 11 analyses say") concludes that Pros still beat individual bots but bots make consistent progress. — [LessWrong synthesis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say)

### Inferences
- **[Strong inference]** A Brier gap of about 0.003 between an LLM and the market (Prophet Arena) is far smaller than typical round-trip costs (spread plus taker fee of about 1–3% near 50¢). Benchmark-level "edge" does not survive as tradeable edge unless it is concentrated in specific mispriced markets or used as a maker.
- **[Speculative]** LLMs' known weakness, slow aggregation near resolution, is exactly where markets are most efficient. LLM edge, if any, is likelier on long-dated, thin, or neglected markets where liquidity is poor and capacity is low.
- **[Speculative]** If FRI's late-2026 parity projection holds, "AI forecaster" will become a commodity rather than a moat.

### Gaps
- No public, audited live-money P&L for an LLM forecasting agent on Polymarket or Kalshi.
- I did not confirm whether ForecastBench parity was reached by Oct 2026, and found no latest 2026 leaderboard figures.
- Fall 2026 Metaculus results are not yet available.

## 4. APIs — Polymarket CLOB / Gamma, Kalshi — capabilities, limits, auth, fees

### Takeaway
Both venues offer full programmatic trading. Polymarket's API is permissionless at the API layer: Gamma, Data and CLOB reads need no auth; trading needs a Polygon wallet with L1 (EIP-712) and L2 (HMAC) credentials, and rate limits are generous. Kalshi requires a KYC'd account and RSA-signed requests, with tiered and much tighter rate limits. Both charge price-dependent taker fees that peak at 50¢ and pay or charge makers little.

### Cited Findings
- **[Verified]** Polymarket auth: the Gamma API, Data API and CLOB read endpoints (orderbook, prices, spreads) need **no authentication**. **L1** signs an EIP-712 struct with the Polygon private key (domain "ClobAuthDomain", chain 137) and is used to create or derive API keys and sign orders. **L2** uses an API key, secret and passphrase with HMAC-SHA256 for trading endpoints. The current SDKs are `@polymarket/clob-client-v2` and `py-clob-client-v2`. — [Polymarket API auth](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/authentication), [CLOB auth](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/CLOB/authentication), [Trading overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/overview), [Mintlify clob-client signing](https://www.mintlify.com/Polymarket/clob-client/api/utilities/signing)
- **[Verified]** Polymarket CLOB rate limits (Cloudflare throttling, which **queues** rather than rejects): general 9,000 req/10s; `/book` and `/price` 1,500/10s; `/books` and `/prices` 500/10s; `/prices-history` 1,000/10s; ledger endpoints 900/10s; API-key endpoints 100/10s; `POST /order` 5,000/10s burst and 48,000/10min sustained. A third-party n8n post cites an older 3,500/10s order burst. — [Polymarket rate limits](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/rate-limits)
- **[Strong]** Polymarket fees in 2026: taker-only fees started on 15-minute crypto (Jan 2026), then all crypto timeframes by 6 Mar 2026, then a broader "Fee Structure V2" from 30 Mar 2026. Sacra reports that V2 sets a crypto taker rate of 0.07, makers pay zero and receive rebates, and geopolitics and world events stay fee-free. The rebate share is about 20% of crypto taker fees, 25% for most categories and 50% for finance, at Polymarket's discretion. A taker-rebate tier programme (Bronze → Obsidian, based on 30-day weighted volume, paid in pUSD) launched around 28–29 May 2026. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384461/polymarket-adds-taker-fees-to-15-minute-crypto-markets-to-fund-liquidity-rebates), [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout), [Sacra](https://www.sacra.com/c/polymarket/), [Polymarket taker rebates](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/taker-rebates). Sacra's 0.07 figure is not confirmed against the live fee page.
- **[Anecdotal]** Pine Analytics found the volume impact of the fees modest: no decline in sports, and a slight crypto pullback within normal variance. — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)
- **[Verified]** Kalshi taker fee = round-up of **0.07 × C × P × (1−P)** (C = contracts, P = price in $), so it peaks at about 1.75¢ per contract at 50¢. A maker fee of 0.0175 × C × P × (1−P) applies only on series that charge makers; the default maker multiplier is 0. The fee-schedule PDF says it was effective 5 Feb 2026, while another source cites a July 2026 revision. — [Kalshi fee schedule PDF](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf?), [Allium: Kalshi fees](https://allium.so/blog/kalshi-fees-why-the-cost-peaks-near-50-cents/), [Allium series fee changes](https://docs.allium.so/historical-data/predictions/kalshi/series-fee-changes.md)
- **[Verified]** Kalshi API rate-limit tiers (read/write per second): **Basic 20/10, Advanced 30/30, Premier 100/100 (≥3.75% of monthly exchange volume), Prime 400/400 (≥7.5%)**. Premier and Prime also require demonstrated API-security competence. An older readme lists 10 TPS / 600 TPM for Basic, which is outdated. — [Kalshi docs: rate limits](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits), [older Kalshi readme](https://trading-api.readme.io/reference/tiers-and-rate-limits)
- **[Anecdotal]** Kalshi auth: requests are signed with RSA-PSS over timestamp + method + path, sent in three headers. API access requires a KYC'd Kalshi account. — [pm.wiki Kalshi API recipes](https://pm.wiki/ja/learn/kalshi-api-python-recipes), [Allium: Kalshi API KYC and fees](https://allium.so/blog/kalshi-api-what-kyc-and-fees-mean-for-access/)

### Inferences
- **[Strong inference]** Fees near 50¢ (Kalshi about 1.75¢ per contract, i.e. 3.5% of a 50¢ stake; Polymarket crypto about 1.5–3%) wipe out most naive taker strategies. Maker-side execution is close to mandatory for any systematic edge.
- **[Speculative]** Polymarket's permissionless wallet-based API makes it technically easy to reach from geoblocked jurisdictions. The geoblock and ToS still apply at the API layer (see section 5). That makes it a legal and ToS problem, not a technical one.

### Gaps
- I could not read docs.polymarket.com live (DNS failure), so I could not confirm the current fee coefficient per category.
- I found no Polymarket US (QCX DCM) API documentation; it is unclear whether Polymarket US offers a separate API.
- I found no WebSocket limits for either venue.

## 5. Legal / access — Polymarket US status, UK access, Kalshi international, insider trading

### Takeaway
For a **UK-based builder, both Polymarket and Kalshi are effectively closed for trading**. The UK Gambling Commission says prediction markets are likely gambling and need a betting-intermediary licence; neither venue holds one, and Polymarket's UK status is reported as close-only. Kalshi's 140-country expansion explicitly excludes the UK. Building tools (data, analytics) is lawful. Operating a UK-facing trading or copy-trading service on these venues would likely be unlicensed gambling facilitation, and could also touch FCA rules. Insider-trading enforcement on event contracts became real in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **[Strong]** Polymarket bought QCEX (a CFTC-licensed DCM and clearinghouse) for about **$112M in July 2025**, creating QCX LLC d/b/a **Polymarket US**. ## 6. Why Polymarket bot content goes viral — notable examples

### Takeaway
Bot virality runs on transparent on-chain P&L (anyone can screenshot a wallet), extreme small-stake-to-large-profit ratios, high "win rates" and an "AI did it" framing. Much of the amplification comes from sites that sell bots, referrals or courses. The canonical viral case (0x8dxd) was latency arbitrage on a market structure that Polymarket then changed.

### Cited Findings
- **[Strong]** The canonical example is wallet 0x8dxd: "$313 → $438k in a month", 98% win rate, about 6,615 trades on 15-minute BTC markets (Dec 2025). Covered by Finbold, Bitget News and MEXC News, and amplified on X. — [Finbold](https://finbold.com/trading-bot-turns-313-into-438000-on-polymarket-in-a-month/), [Bitget News](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605137629), [X post](https://x.com/0xPhilanthrop/status/2037846757447458964)

### Inferences
- **[Strong inference]** The virality drivers are (1) public on-chain verifiability of winners only (survivorship), (2) tiny-stake, huge-multiple narratives, (3) "AI/Claude did it" branding riding the agent hype, and (4) a commercial incentive: many sources sell bots, courses or affiliate access. Against this, 70–88% of wallets lose money and most bots lose (section 2).
- **[Speculative]** Prediction-market odds as an **information product** for agents and analysts (signals, dashboards, insider-flow alerts) has broader appeal and fewer legal problems for a UK builder than a trading bot.
