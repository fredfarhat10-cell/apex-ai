# Picks-and-Shovels Businesses Selling Tools/Data to Niche-Market Traders (as of Oct 2026)

Evidence labels used throughout: **[Verified]** = primary/company or reputable trade press, multiple consistent sources; **[Strong]** = credible single source or consistent secondary sources; **[Anecdotal]** = review/affiliate sites, social posts, self-reported; **[Speculative]** = inference/estimate-model output; **[Unknown]** = not found.

Research note: ~16 tool calls, mostly web search summaries. Many pricing figures come from affiliate/review sites that often compete with the vendors they review. Revenue data for small vendors is almost never disclosed. Aggregator estimates (GetLatka, ZoomInfo, Tracxn, NorthMetric) are model outputs, not disclosures.

## Q1. Examples: traction, pricing, revenue

### Takeaway
The best documented exit in the space is OddsJam: bootstrapped, about $26M projected 2024 revenue, sold to Gambling.com Group for $80M upfront plus up to $80M earnout. That proves sports-betting odds and data tooling can reach far above the founder's £300k–£5m target. Most other niche tools (Polymarket trackers, Betfair software, LEGO/watch trackers) price at $5–$30/month, and their revenue is undisclosed or estimated in the hundreds of thousands. DEX Screener is an outlier: it makes tens of millions a year from token-promotion fees, not trader subscriptions.

### Inferences
- [Speculative] Price bands cluster by niche. Sharp sports-betting tools charge $99–$800/mo because users bet large sums and the edge is measurable. Collectibles and Polymarket tools charge $5–$30/mo. Betfair ladders charge £6–£15/mo. To reach £300k ARR, that means about 125 users at a $200/mo sports-betting price, versus about 2,500–5,000 users at a $5–10/mo Polymarket-tracker price.
- [Speculative] The two documented exits (OddsJam to Gambling.com; Card Ladder to Collectors) were both bought by a large operator or marketplace in the same vertical. A strategic acquirer is the realistic exit path, not an IPO.
- [Speculative] The highest-revenue model in crypto analytics (DEX Screener) monetises the *listed projects*, not the traders. Charging the supply side for visibility can far outearn subscriptions.

- I did not fetch Companies House filings for UK vendors (Bet Angel Ltd, Outplayed Ltd, OddsMonkey). That is the most promising next step for UK revenue clues.

## Q2. Which are small teams with meaningful revenue?

### Takeaway
Only OddsJam has confirmed, meaningful revenue from a lean, bootstrapped team ($26M projected 2024 revenue at a 46% EBITDA margin). Card Ladder (2-founder origin) had a strategic exit at undisclosed terms. HypurrScan is a confirmed solo/bootstrapped Hyperliquid explorer but is free. For other candidates there is indirect evidence of a small team (bootstrapped, founder-led, long-running) but no revenue data.

### Cited Findings
- [Strong] OddsJam was bootstrapped (no funding rounds) and projected $26M revenue / $12M adj. EBITDA for 2024. — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2024/12/12/gamblingcom-buying-oddsjam-parent/); [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/oddsjam/__YVZl2cTvMbk8PkYfyaqzfyHqYfMlFSJpVttQhUrqStA)
- [Verified] Card Ladder was founded by two people (Johnson, McGill) and sold to Collectors Universe in 2021. — [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/commerce/2021/collectors-universe-buys-card-ladder-1234647703/)
- [Strong] PriceCharting has been run by one founder since 2007 and has a sister site, SportsCardPro. This is a long-lived, founder-led niche data business; revenue is unknown. — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jjhendricks/)
- [Anecdotal] HypurrScan is built by one developer ("Louis"/"Syavel"), is bootstrapped, free and has no token. It became the de facto Hyperliquid explorer. — [PMWisdom](https://pmwisdom.com/projects/hypurrscan); [Hypurr.co](https://www.hypurr.co/ecosystem-projects/hypurrscan)
- [Anecdotal] HyperDash started as an independent analytics dashboard and was acquired by pvp.trade, which rebuilt it as a trading terminal. - [Anecdotal, self-reported] OddsMonkey has run since 2010 and claims 400k users. — [ProfitDuel](https://www.profitduel.com/profitduel-and-oddsmonkey)
- [Speculative] WatchCharts' estimated ~$550K ARR, if accurate, is a small-team number. It may have shut down. — [GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/atchcharts.com)

### Inferences
- [Speculative] In crypto ecosystems (Hyperliquid), standalone analytics gets acquired into execution venues and terminals that earn on trading fees. Free analytics is the funnel; execution is the business. That makes pure-subscription analytics hard to sustain there.
- [Speculative] The founder's £300k–£5m target sits between most hobbyist tools (well under £1m) and OddsJam ($26M). UK Betfair/matched-betting vendors (Bet Angel, OddsMonkey, Outplayed) are the most plausible comparables for that band, but this is unconfirmed.

### Gaps

## Q3. Which niches have growing participants but weak tools?

### Takeaway
The clearest new UK opportunity is regulated prediction markets. Matchbook launched UK predictions under its UKGC exchange licence (around early 2026), and IG Group applied for a Gambling Commission betting licence in Oct 2026 for an in-house predictions product. I found no third-party tooling for these venues yet. Polymarket tools, by contrast, are already crowded and cheap. CS2 is volatile and Valve-dependent.

### Cited Findings
- [Strong] Matchbook planned to be the first UK-regulated prediction-markets operator, used the UK as a "road test" before the US, and focused first on sports outcomes (Dec 2025 reporting). — [SBC News](https://sbcnews.co.uk/featurednews/2025/12/12/matchbook-predictions-uk-us/); [Sigma](https://sigma.world/news/matchbook-uk-prediction-market-launch-us-expansion/); [Betting.co.uk](https://www.betting.co.uk/news/legal/matchbook-will-become-first-uk-regulated-prediction-markets-operator/)

## Q4. Business model norms

### Takeaway
The norm is freemium plus a monthly subscription, with an annual discount (about 17–50%), and an API tier priced 5–20x the consumer tier. Crypto tools add "lifetime" crypto payments and execution fees. DEX Screener charges listed projects. In gambling, matched betting and odds screens depend on bookmaker relationships and affiliate links, which brings legal and regulatory exposure.

### Cited Findings
- [Strong] Annual discounts: Unabated 17%; RebelBetting Starter $99 down to $69 (30%); Polywhaler "save 49% vs monthly"; Card Ladder $20/mo vs $200/yr. — [BetHero](https://betherosports.com/blog/unabated-alternative); [RebelBetting](https://www.rebelbetting.com/pricing); [Polywhaler](https://polywhaler.com/); [Card Ladder](https://www.cardladder.com/pricing)
- [Strong] API/data tiers: Pricempire API $119.90–$239.90/mo; Polywhaler Quant (API) $359–$599/yr against $59/yr for Pro. — [Pricempire API](https://pricempire.com/api); [OrcaLayer](https://orcalayer.com/blog/polymarket-whale-trackers-compared)
- [Strong] B2B data licensing is a value driver: OddsJam's odds-data platform (about 300 books) was a key part of the Gambling.com purchase. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/gambling-com-rolls-dice-oddsjams-160218004.html)
- [Anecdotal] Crypto-native pricing: Polymarket Analytics offers "1 ETH lifetime". HyperDash is free and earns trade-execution fees. — [Polymonit](https://polymonit.com/blog/best-polymarket-tools-2026); [Truehold](https://www.truehold.xyz/blog/best-hyperliquid-tools)
- [Strong] DEX Screener charges token issuers for promotion (about $32M annualised per DefiLlama at the time of the check). — [DefiLlama](https://defillama.com/protocol/dex-screener)
- [Strong] Gambling.com Group is an affiliate-marketing company. Buying OddsJam shows affiliates value subscription-tool revenue as diversification. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling.com_Group)

### Inferences
- [Speculative] For a UK founder, affiliate revenue from bookmakers (CPA/rev-share) carries UKGC exposure: operators are responsible for their affiliates' marketing, and CAP/ASA gambling-advertising rules apply. Promoting trading or prediction products that the FCA might treat as financial promotions could trigger s21 FSMA. Not verified in this research; needs legal review.

### Gaps
- No sourced material was found on UKGC or FCA rules for affiliates or tool vendors; this needs a dedicated search or legal advice.
- No churn, conversion or ARPU data for any product.

## Q5. Moats and failure modes

### Takeaway
The main failure modes are platform-owner actions (Valve game updates and ToS enforcement, venue API or ToS changes), legal action by operators (Entain vs. OddsMonkey/Outplayed), commoditisation (dozens of near-identical Polymarket and Hyperliquid tools, many free), and absorption into execution venues. The durable moats seen are: becoming the cited data authority (Pricempire market cap, Card Ladder indices), owning a broad proprietary data feed (OddsJam's 300 books), and founder or brand trust (Bet Angel's Peter Webb).

### Cited Findings
- [Verified] Valve has a history of enforcement: in 2016 it sent cease-and-desist letters to 23 skin-gambling sites, saying Steam is licensed "for personal, non-commercial use only", and banned some sites from Steam API login. In 2023 it made "gambling" a bannable offence. — [GosuGamers](https://www.gosugamers.net/dota2/news/40463-valve-issue-cease-and-desist-papers-to-twenty-three-gambling-websites); [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/news/2249/valve-and-twitch-deal-double-blow-to-skin-gambling)
- [Strong] In Dec 2025 Valve's tournament rules barred case-opening and skin-trading sites from tournament broadcasts, which cut off a sponsorship marketing channel. — [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/counter-strike/guides/cs2-skin-sponsorship-ban)
- [Anecdotal] In Sep 2026 Valve reportedly sent a cease-and-desist to a large skinchanger site. — [EsportNow](https://esportnow.gg/cs2/news/valve-shuts-down-massive-cs2-skinchanger-website-touch-skins)
- [Verified] Valve game-design changes directly reprice the underlying market (Oct 2025 trade-up crash, $1.7–2B; trade-protection drop, $615M). — [Dust2.us](https://www.dust2.us/news/66949/cs2-skins-market-craters-17-billion-after-october-22nd-update-throws-market-into-chaos); [Lis-Skins](https://lis-skins.com/blog/steam-trade-protection/)
- [Strong] Operator legal risk: Entain's High Court claim against OddsMonkey and Outplayed (brand and copyright). — [Gaming Awards](https://gaming-awards.com/NEWS/entains-legal-battle-against-matched-betting-companies/)
- [Strong] Pricempire's data is quoted as the market authority in mainstream esports press. — [Dust2.us](https://www.dust2.us/news/66949/cs2-skins-market-craters-17-billion-after-october-22nd-update-throws-market-into-chaos)
- [Anecdotal] Commoditisation: about 22 Hyperliquid analytics tools, several free (HypurrScan, ASXN HyperScreener, HyperDash), and many Polymarket trackers at $5–$20/mo. — [HL Hub](https://x.com/Hyperliquid_Hub/status/1926981361602007067); [Polymonit](https://polymonit.com/blog/best-polymarket-tools-2026)
- [Anecdotal] Third parties sell scrapers for Card Ladder's index (Apify), so scraped data can itself be re-scraped and the moat leaks. — [Apify](https://apify.com/jungle_synthesizer/cardladder-graded-card-price-index-scraper)

### Inferences
- [Speculative] Tools built on official, documented APIs (Betfair Exchange API, Polymarket's public CLOB/Gamma APIs, Hyperliquid's public API) carry less legal risk than tools that scrape marketplaces (Steam, eBay, sportsbooks). Each venue's ToS still needs checking.
- [Speculative] A tool for a single new venue (e.g. Matchbook Predictions) depends heavily on that venue's API policy and survival. Covering several venues (Betfair + Matchbook + Smarkets + IG + Polymarket/Kalshi as reference prices) would hedge this.

### Gaps
- Polymarket's ToS for third-party data use or redistribution and its API rate limits were not checked.
- Matchbook and IG API availability for third-party tools is unknown.
- No case law on scraping legality (UK/EU database right, US CFAA/hiQ) was gathered in this pass.
